Answers to the experiment questions

  1. Fastest decisions: Jev 1.13, 0.782 s per move on average.
  2. Highest solve rate: none. Every model solved 0 of 5 puzzles within 30 moves.
  3. Shortest solutions: no model solved a puzzle, so no optimality gap could be measured.
  4. Did the fastest model give the best quality? No. Jev 1.13 solved 0/5.
  5. Recommendation: Speed alone did not help. The three decision models answered in about 0.8-1.4 s per move but solved 0 of 15 puzzles in 30 moves each, although every puzzle needed only 4. Jev and Clef mostly repeated or undid moves (U U′ U U′, U2 U2 U2), and Luna refused all 150 requests. No frontier LLM produced a move (credit, mandatory reasoning cost and rate limits), so that side is unmeasured. An exact search solved every puzzle optimally in milliseconds on a CPU. I would use a hybrid: search or a planner for multi-step solving, and a fast decision model only for cheap single-step classification and gating.
