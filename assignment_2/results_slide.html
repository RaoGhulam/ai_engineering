Answers to the experiment questions

  1. Fastest decisions: Jev 1.13, 0.828 s per move on average.
  2. Highest solve rate: none. Every model solved 0 of 3 puzzles within the move limit.
  3. Shortest solutions: no model solved a puzzle, so no optimality gap could be measured. The BFS optimum was 4 moves for each puzzle.
  4. Did the fastest model give the best quality? No. Jev 1.13 was fastest but looped U, U′, U, …; speed bought no progress.
  5. Recommendation: On this evidence, neither raw System-1 decision models nor an unmeasured frontier LLM should drive a cube agent alone. The three decision models answered in about 0.8-1.5 s per move but solved 0 of 9 puzzles. Jev and Clef fell into undo loops (U, U′, U, ...), and their confidences (0.003-0.47 across 18 options) were mostly low and never pointed toward progress. Luna refused 14 of 15 requests. No frontier or free LLM returned a single move (credit, reasoning cost and rate limits blocked all four attempts), so the frontier side is unmeasured. An exact search solved every puzzle optimally in milliseconds on the CPU. I would use a hybrid: deterministic search for the multi-step planning, and a fast decision model only for cheap, single-step classification and gating.
